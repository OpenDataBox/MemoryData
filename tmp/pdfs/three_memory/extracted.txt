LEARNING TO REMEMBER FROM FAILURE :
KNOWLEDGE , E XPERIENCE , AND WORKING MEMORY
Anonymous authors
Manuscript in preparation
ABSTRACT
Conversational memory can fail because evidence was discarded, relevant records
were not retrieved, or retrieved evidence was used incorrectly. Repeating the same
memory pipeline does not distinguish these failures. We propose a framework
with two persistent banks and one transient working state: a knowledge bank
stores source-grounded facts, an experience bank stores reusable repair proce-
dures, and working memory tracks the evidence still needed for the current ques-
tion. Experience controls how knowledge is encoded, retrieved, and used; only the
knowledge bank can supply factual support for an answer. Failed development tra-
jectories produce candidate procedures, which are retained only after controlled
replay and validation. The model weights remain ﬁxed. We specify three fo-
cused experiments on LoCoMo and LongMemEval: answer quality and cost, the
contribution of the three memory roles, and transfer as experience accumulates.
This manuscript presents the method and experimental protocol; benchmark re-
sults have not yet been measured.
1 I NTRODUCTION
A memory-augmented agent may answer a question about a user’s current residence with an old
address. Three different failures can produce that answer: the move was omitted during memory
construction, the new address was stored but not retrieved, or both addresses were retrieved without
comparing their dates. These failures call for different interventions. An extra search cannot recover
information that was never retained, while a better writer cannot by itself resolve a reader’s temporal
mistake.
Existing systems make complementary advances in memory extraction, organization, and efﬁcient
retrieval (Chhikara et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2025). Experience-based
agents reuse feedback and prior attempts ( Shinn et al. , 2023; Zhang et al. , 2026; Cao et al. , 2025).
Y et a conversational memory system still needs an explicit connection between what it remembers
and what it has learned about remembering. We study that connection through three roles: persistent
knowledge, persistent experience, and transient working state.
The proposed mechanism is failure-directed memory repair. A trace records which facts were avail-
able, what was retrieved, and which evidence supported the answer. A learner tests a small repair to
the writer, retriever, or reader and distills a successful intervention into a reusable conditional pro-
cedure. Future questions retrieve these procedures as guidance, while their factual answers remain
grounded in the current conversation’s knowledge bank. Working memory connects the two banks
by exposing missing evidence and failed attempts.
The memory taxonomy itself is established ( LangChain, 2026). Recuris already uses working state
to select experience and localizes failures for memory-control updates ( Y u et al., 2026). Our pro-
posed distinction is the repair of a separate factual bank and its evidence-access pipeline for conver-
sational QA. This distinction is a hypothesis to test, rather than a claim of demonstrated superiority.
The contribution of this draft is a concrete architecture, a bounded repair procedure, and an evalua-
tion that separates factual recall from cross-question procedural transfer.

2 T HREE MEMORY ROLES
Let Hi be the timestamped dialogue history associated with question qi. The agent uses a frozen
language model πθ and memory
Mi,t = (Ki, En, wi,t), (1)
where Ki is scoped to the history, En contains experience accepted after development stage n, and
wi,t exists only during the current question. Histories belonging to the same conversation may reuse
their knowledge bank; unrelated users or evaluation folds cannot share facts. Figure 1 shows the two
update timescales.
2.1 K NOWLEDGE : P RESERVE CLAIMS AND THEIR SOURCES
A knowledge record contains a claim, its participants, temporal scope, source turn identiﬁers, and a
version. The same bank retains the original source records for bounded inspection or re-encoding. A
writer processes history in chronological batches without access to future questions or gold answers.
It preserves dated changes instead of replacing every old claim with the latest one. A correction links
the revised claim to the affected version; incompatible claims remain explicit until their scopes can
be resolved.
The default index combines dense and lexical retrieval. Accepted encoding procedures may change
the writer’s extraction instructions, such as preserving the time of a state change or retaining both
participants in a relation. These procedures are selected from properties of the incoming batch, not
from an unseen evaluation question. At query time, a bounded source inspection may recover an
omitted distinction. Such access is logged and charged to the retrieval and context budgets.
2.2 E XPERIENCE : S TORE CONDITIONAL REPAIR PROCEDURES
An experience entry has the form
e = (c, z, p, u), z ∈ {encode, retrieve, answer}, (2)
where c speciﬁes its applicability, z names the operation to change, p is a short procedure, and
u records validation support and subsequent successes or failures. For example, a procedure may
require searching adjacent dated sessions when a question asks about a change of state. It must not
store a benchmark answer as the procedure. Names, answer strings, and conversation identiﬁers are
removed from reusable guidance; development source identiﬁers remain in an audit record that is
not exposed as answer context.
Applicability is ﬁltered by operation and observable problem features before semantic ranking. Re-
trieval returns at most two procedures. A validated procedure has priority over an untested candi-
date, and a contradicted or repeatedly unhelpful entry is retired. This bank transfers problem-solving

2.3 W ORKING STATE: T RACK EVIDENCE AND UNRESOLVED NEEDS
Working memory is a compact record of the question, evidence identiﬁers, unresolved entities or
dates, conﬂicts, attempted operations, and remaining budget. It is initialized for each question and
discarded afterward. The agent does not persist a free-form reasoning transcript as factual memory.
At step t, the controller retrieves applicable experience and updates the working state from the actual
retrieval or inspection result:
Pt = RetrieveExperience(En, qi, wi,t),
(Rt, at) = Act( πθ, qi, wi,t, Pt, Ki),
wi,t+1 = Update( wi,t, Rt, at).
(3)
Available actions are knowledge search, source inspection, comparison of retrieved evidence, and
answer or abstain. A missing fact is marked resolved only when a returned source supports it. The
agent stops when the evidence is sufﬁcient or the action budget is exhausted. Experience may guide
the search or interpretation, but cannot itself serve as factual evidence.
3 L EARNING FROM FAILURE
Development runs log the initial knowledge version, retrieved records, working states, actions, cited
sources, answer, and feedback. Gold answers and evidence annotations may supervise this devel-
opment process; they are unavailable during held-out inference. A failure opens a small, predeﬁned
repair space:
Repair target Diagnostic evidence Candidate intervention
Encoding Relevant source exists, but its claim or scope
is missing from the bank

The learner proposes one local procedure for each implicated target and replays the failed devel-
opment examples with the model, sources, and resource limits held ﬁxed. A probe changes one
operation at a time. Its outcome indicates a useful intervention, not a uniquely identiﬁed causal
explanation; ambiguous failures may remain unresolved. An encoding repair must produce source-
backed records, and an answering repair cannot inject the gold answer into the prompt.
Let F be the failed development batch and V a separate validation set. A candidate procedure p is
admitted only when it improves mean QA score on F , does not reduce mean QA score on V , and
respects the resource limits:
Accept(p) = 1{∆F (p) > 0 ∧ ∆V (p) ≥ 0 ∧ C(p) ≤ B}. (4)
Replays use matched seeds and starting knowledge snapshots. A failed candidate does not alter
deployed experience. The writer is versioned with the accepted encoding procedures so that future
banks can be rebuilt from their histories. Within a frozen evaluation run, source inspection changes
only the current working context; answering one question cannot improve the bank for the next.
The loop is bounded to three development rounds and a bank of at most 64 procedures. It retains
generalized guidance rather than an ever-growing set of answer examples. The learner receives vali-
dation acceptance decisions, while test histories, questions, labels, and scores never enter procedure
generation or selection. This is continual adaptation through external memory; no model parameters
are optimized.

4 E XPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL
Empirical status. The experiments below are speciﬁed but not executed. No accuracy, cost reduc-
tion, signiﬁcance claim, or synthetic performance curve is reported as an observed result.
Benchmarks and separation. We will evaluate the released LoCoMo conversational QA data
(Maharana et al. , 2024) and the cleaned LongMemEval-S data ( Wu et al. , 2024). LoCoMo tests
single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, open-domain, and adversarial questions; LongMemEval tests long
histories, updates, temporal reasoning, and cross-session recall. The primary protocol uses ﬁve
outer folds. Each LoCoMo fold assigns six whole conversations to experience development, two
to validation, and two to test; each conversation is tested once. LongMemEval uses approximately
60/20/20 percent development/validation/test per fold, grouping examples with shared user histories
or source sessions before assignment. Every released example is evaluated only in its held-out fold.
Exact group sizes and manifests must be published. These are cross-ﬁtted research scores, not the
ofﬁcial ﬁxed-bank leaderboard setting.
Each test history is ingested from scratch using the frozen experience learned outside its fold. Nei-
ther benchmark’s test feedback updates either bank. Chronological batches within the development
partition supply the experience stream; this ordering is an experimental schedule, not a claim that
unrelated benchmark histories form one natural user timeline.
Shared conﬁguration and comparison. The planned primary reader and learner are Qwen3-8B,
with Qwen3-Embedding-4B for dense retrieval. Temperature is zero and paired trials use three
seeds. The primary per-step memory budget is 4,096 reader tokens, allocated as up to 3,072 for
evidence, 512 for experience, and 512 for working state. Unused space is available to evidence. A
question permits at most three retrieval or inspection actions; the same total memory budget and
action ceiling apply to controlled baselines. We will report the 2,048/8,192-token budgets only as a
sensitivity curve, and cap experience development at three rounds.
Comparisons include raw-history hybrid RAG, Mem0, A-MEM, SimpleMem, LightMem, and a
LangMem store manager. Every method uses the same reader and embedding models. We will pin
source commits and report any adapters or deviations from native execution; a modiﬁed implemen-
tation will not be labeled a paper reproduction. Full-context answering is a separate reference with
its larger input budget explicitly reported. An additional controlled EM–WM baseline uses experi-
ence and working state with ﬁxed knowledge encoding, inspired by Recuris; it is an adaptation for
QA, not an ofﬁcial Recuris result.
4.1 A NSWER QUALITY AND COST
The main comparison will report LoCoMo token F1 over the four answerable categories, category-
level scores, and adversarial abstention separately. LongMemEval uses the ofﬁcial question-type-
aware judging procedure. A pinned GPT-4o judge is planned, with its exact snapshot, prompt,
and raw decisions recorded; the same judge is used across systems. Evidence recall is reported
from source turns for LoCoMo and supporting sessions for LongMemEval, with abstention cases
excluded from evidence recall. These units are not pooled. An experience entry never counts as
retrieved factual support.
One main results table will pair answer quality with retrieved tokens, median and tail latency, and
total model calls. Construction, development replay, and query-time costs will be shown separately,
alongside amortized cost per answered question. Paired uncertainty estimates will resample entire
conversations or shared-history groups, rather than treating correlated questions as independent.
4.2 C ONTRIBUTION OF THE THREE MEMORIES
A single controlled ablation table will compare the full framework with: knowledge only; knowl-
edge plus experience with no persistent working state; knowledge plus working state with no learned
experience; and one pooled store containing the same knowledge and procedure records. All vari-
ants keep the same source archive, action ceiling, reader, and total token budget; removed memory
allocations return to factual evidence. A ﬁxed, length-matched set of human-written procedures tests
whether learned experience helps beyond generic advice. We will also disable encoding repair while

4.3 C ONTINUAL TRANSFER AND MEMORY GROWTH
We will evaluate frozen experience snapshots after each development round on untouched test folds
without selecting a snapshot by test score. A single curve will show answer quality against accumu-
lated experience and total development cost; a companion retention measure will track previously
solved validation questions. A cross-benchmark test will learn experience on LoCoMo and apply it
to LongMemEval, and reverse the direction, rebuilding all target knowledge banks from their own
histories without target feedback. This separates procedural transfer from memorizing a bench-
mark’s answers. Source-side validation ﬁxes all settings before the transfer run.
5 R ELATED WORK AND SCOPE
Fact-oriented systems extract, connect, and consolidate information for later recall, including Mem0,
A-MEM, SimpleMem, and LightMem ( Chhikara et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026; Fang
et al., 2025). Our knowledge bank adopts this setting; the proposed addition is learning conditional
interventions from its failures. Reﬂexion, AdaMEM, and ReMe reuse feedback or execution experi-
ence (Shinn et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2026; Cao et al., 2025). LangMem exposes semantic, episodic,
and procedural memory as conﬁgurable primitives ( LangChain, 2026).
Recuris is the closest control-level comparison: it couples experience with veriﬁed working state and
updates skills, state speciﬁcations, invocation policies, and checkers from failed traces ( Y u et al.,
2026). We do not claim these general principles as new. Our speciﬁc target is factual omission
and evidence access in a separately scoped conversational knowledge bank. The encoding-repair
ablation and the ﬁxed-encoding EM–WM control are necessary to determine whether that distinction
has measurable value.
6 L IMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION
The design depends on the ﬁdelity of source attribution and development feedback. Replay can
identify a helpful repair without proving the true cause of failure. Repeated validation may overﬁt a
small development set, and LoCoMo has few independent conversations. Keeping source records in-
creases storage cost; learned procedures may also transfer poorly across domains. These limitations
must be evaluated rather than assumed away.
The framework separates factual support, reusable experience, and current evidence state. Its central
hypothesis is that experience should change how knowledge is retained and accessed, while remain-
ing distinct from the facts used to answer. LoCoMo and LongMemEval provide a focused test of
that hypothesis. Until those experiments are completed, effectiveness remains an open empirical
question.
